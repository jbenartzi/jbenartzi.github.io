Exercise Rules Exercises are available in the Timeline page. Assignments of Week n are due on Week n+1. Assignments should be handed in, in-person, to the TA.

Assignments must include your name, and must be:
  • Hand written
  • Clearly written
  • Concise
  • Stapled

Late submissions will not be accepted.

You may work alone or in groups, as long as you make sure you're learning and understanding what you're writing. This is for your own good! You won't learn anything if you copy from Google or ChatGPT. Also, these exercises are very likely to appear in the exam (both in the written and the oral parts).
General Exam Structure
 There are six exam sessions:
  • Two in winter 2027
  • Two in summer 2027
  • Two in fall 2027

Each session will have three parts:

  1. Written Part A
  2. Written Part B
  3. Oral

All three parts must be completed successfully within the same session in order to pass the course.
To be admitted to Written Part B, you must pass Written Part A.
To be admitted to the Oral exam, you must pass Written Part B.
Written Exam Rules

Before the exam

You must register for the exam on Delphi by the registration deadline. Students who are not registered will not be admitted.

You must arrive 30 minutes prior to the start of the exam and wait outside the room.

Your ID will be checked at the entrance and you will sign an attendance sheet. I may assign seats, and I may move you to another seat during the exam.

Accommodations

Students with disabilities or specific learning disorders (DSA) who need accommodations (e.g. extra time) must contact CARIS and me well before the exam.

On your desk

You may only have the following on your desk:

  • Pen/pencil/eraser
  • Water
  • ID

You may not have:

  • Electronic devices (no phones, no headphones, no calculators, no smart watches)
  • Books
  • Notes

All other items (including coats, jackets and hats) must be in your backpack at the side of the room.

Phones and all other devices must be switched off (not on silent) and kept in your backpack. Having a device on you during the exam, even switched off, counts as a violation. This includes earbuds and smart glasses.

Exam paper

Use only the paper provided. Write your name and student number on every sheet. At the end, hand in all sheets, including scratch paper. You may not take the exam text out of the room or photograph it.

Leaving the room

You may not leave the room during the first hour and during the last 30 minutes of the exam.

Outside these times you may use the toilet. You must leave your phone and all other belongings in your bag. The time you spend out of the room is recorded. If you spend an unreasonable amount of time out of the room, this will be noted, and you will face more questions at the oral exam (if you qualify).

Cheating

No talking from the moment you enter the room until you have handed in your exam.

Copying from or communicating with another student, looking at another student's work, or using any unauthorized material is strictly forbidden. Any cheating, or suspected cheating, will be noted.

Artificial intelligence

The use of AI tools (chatbots, AI assistants or anything similar) during the exam is strictly forbidden, including during toilet breaks. This is in addition to the ban on phones, smart watches and other electronic devices.

End of the exam

When time is called, stop writing immediately and remain seated until your exam has been collected. Writing after time is called will be noted.

You may withdraw at any time after the first hour. A withdrawn exam is not graded and does not count as an attempt.

After the exam

You may view your corrected written exam on request, at a time I will announce.
Oral Exam Rules

Invitation and time slots

In the days after the written exam I will announce (on the website) who is invited to the oral exam. You will be assigned a specific time slot. You must arrive at least 15 minutes before your assigned time. Bring your ID.

If you cannot attend during your time slot, it is your responsibility to swap with another classmate. I will not be able to accommodate requests for time changes.

If you do not attend the oral exam you will have to take a new written exam.

What the oral exam tests

The oral exam will test your understanding of the material, the ideas and concepts. You will not be required to perform complicated calculations. It will often require you to explain how you solved various problems in the homework assignments or in the written part of the exam. If it is clear that you do not understand, you might receive a negative grade.

The oral exam will last 20-30 minutes.

If your written exam was noted

If your written exam was noted for cheating, suspected cheating, use of AI, or an unreasonable amount of time out of the room, the oral exam will be more thorough: it will be longer and will include more questions on your written solutions and on the homework assignments. Serious cases of cheating may also be reported to the department.

Your grade

After the exam I will tell you if you have passed or failed. If you have passed, I will tell you your grade. Grading will follow the grading guidelines (see below).

If you are happy with your grade, you will sign your name to confirm. This will determine your grade, and it cannot be altered. Only in exceptional circumstances will you be allowed to try to improve a grade in the next call. You may refuse the grade and try to retake the exam at a later call. If you fail, you can attend the next call.

The oral examination is a public, transparent event.
Course Grading The maximal grade is 30, the passing grade is 18. Exceptionally good performance will be awarded a 30 e lode (i.e. 30 + honors).
  1. Written Part A: Worth 10 points. To pass Part A you need a minimum of 7 (out of 10) points.
  2. Written Part B: Worth 15 points. To pass Part B you need a minimum of 7 (out of 15) points.
  3. Oral Exam: The grade of the oral exam is between -25 and 5.
  4. Written Part Adjustment: Following the oral exam, the grade of the written exam may be adjusted (up or down by up to 3 points).
  5. Homework: The satisfactory completion of all homework assignments is worth up to 3 points.

Your final grade will be the sum of the final written exam grade, the oral exam grade and the homework grade.

A grade that exceeds 30 will translate to a 30 e lode (honors).
